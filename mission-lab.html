01 / THE BASICS

What is SPHEREx?

SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope designed to survey the entire sky in near-infrared light. Instead of recording just one broad image colour, it measures the sky in 102 wavelength bands, giving scientists information about the light coming from stars, galaxies and interstellar material.

01 · INFRARED VISION

See beyond visible light

Infrared light has wavelengths longer than the human eye can see. Observing it helps scientists study objects and regions that are difficult to investigate using visible light alone.

02 · SPECTRAL MAP

102 bands, not 102 cameras

Each band measures a different part of the near-infrared spectrum. Together, these measurements help reveal clues about the materials and sources producing the light.

03 · WHOLE-SKY SURVEY

Build a map of the sky

SPHEREx repeatedly surveys the whole sky. Repeated coverage builds a broad, consistent dataset that researchers can use to study the universe on large scales.

02 / SPECTRAL COVERAGE

One sky, 102 infrared bands.

The bands span approximately 0.75 to 5 micrometres. The six ranges below are broad guide bands for understanding the instrument's coverage, not a complete list of all 102 individual channels.

Quick clarification: These are infrared wavelengths, not ordinary visible colours. The images shown by COSMOS are display renderings of archive data; their colours or brightness can be adjusted to make image structure easier to see.
03 / SCIENCE GOALS

What questions can it help answer?

SPHEREx was designed to create a wide-field spectral map that supports research into several major questions.

COSMIC HISTORY

How did the universe evolve?

Large-scale measurements of galaxies help researchers investigate the history and distribution of matter across the universe.

GALAXIES

How do galaxies form and change?

Spectral information across the sky helps scientists study populations of galaxies and their properties over cosmic time.

OUR GALAXY

What are the ingredients for life?

Infrared observations of our Milky Way can help researchers map water ice and other molecules in interstellar clouds.
